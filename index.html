1. About this policy

This policy explains how the current Android version of 30 Sec Chaos handles information. The publisher and privacy contact are identified above. An iOS release may be added later; its SDKs, permissions and disclosures must be reviewed before release.

30 Sec Chaos is an offline-first game with no user account and no custom game backend. This does not mean that no personal data is collected. Google Firebase Analytics and Google AdMob / Google Mobile Ads can process identifiers, usage information and other device-related data.

2. Game data stored on your device

The game uses AsyncStorage to save progress and preferences on your device. It is not a dedicated encrypted storage vault. There is no custom game server that synchronizes your saved progress.

Saved information includes best score, total games, total score, coins, daily challenge results by date (score and completion), sound and haptic settings, completed-game count, cooldown end time, and the most recent completed game's score, coins, maximum combo, normal/daily mode, new-best status and rewarded-bonus claim status.

The cooldown and reward-claim records are used locally to control gameplay access and avoid claiming the same reward twice. The current code does not send these local cooldown records to a custom server. Some gameplay statistics are separately included in Analytics events as explained below. Coins are in-game progress, not a payment account.

3. Firebase Analytics

The Android app uses Google Analytics for Firebase to understand app use, gameplay engagement and preferences. Analytics collection is not explicitly disabled or gated by a consent choice in the current implementation. Ad privacy choices collected through UMP do not automatically control all Firebase Analytics processing. Publisher action item: review the intended release regions, Analytics features and legal basis, and implement any separately required Analytics consent or opt-out controls before distribution. Events may be queued by the SDK while offline and transmitted when a connection is available.

The app requests the following events. Actual reporting also depends on Firebase SDK rules and project configuration. In particular, Firebase has automatically collected and reserved event names; a call in the app is not proof that a separate custom event was accepted.

  • app_open: opening the app; no custom parameters.
  • session_start: starting a game session, with mode (normal or daily). Firebase may also collect its own session_start event.
  • daily_challenge_started and daily_challenge_completed: no custom parameters.
  • game_abandoned: leaving an unfinished game, with mode.
  • challenge_started, challenge_completed and challenge_failed: challenge_type and difficulty.
  • session_complete: mode, score, max_combo, coins_earned and difficulty_reached.
  • replay_tapped and share_tapped: no custom parameters.
  • sound_toggled and haptics_toggled: enabled (true or false).

4. Information collected by Google's SDKs

In addition to the app's event parameters, Firebase Analytics may automatically process app-instance identifiers, device or advertising identifiers where available, app and operating-system information, device characteristics, language, session and engagement information, and approximate geographic information derived from network information such as IP addresses. The app does not set a custom account User ID or submit names, email addresses or phone numbers in its gameplay event parameters.

Google Mobile Ads documents collection and sharing of IP addresses (which may estimate general location), app/ad interactions such as launches, taps and video views, diagnostic/performance information, and device or account-related identifiers such as Android advertising ID and app set ID, where available. Collection depends on SDK version, device settings, provider configuration and any applicable consent requirements.

Identifiers can make data relate to a device or app installation even though the game has no sign-in. This policy does not describe SDK data as completely anonymous. See the provider links below for their current collection and processing descriptions.

5. Advertising and rewarded ads

The game integrates AdMob rewarded video ads on Android. Explicitly configured production builds use the publisher's Double Coins rewarded-ad unit. Development/debug builds, development clients and preview builds use Google's test rewarded-ad unit. Missing or inconsistent release ad configuration disables ad requests rather than substituting a test unit. Test ads still communicate with Google's SDKs and do not guarantee zero data collection.

Where the rewarded option is offered, you can choose to watch an ad to earn an additional coin bonus and clear that completed game's cooldown. The reward is granted only when the SDK reports that it was earned. Closing an ad early or an ad failure does not earn the bonus. You may instead wait for the local cooldown. Choosing not to watch is not an Analytics opt-out.

The Android app requests updated privacy information through Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) at startup and asks the SDK to show a consent form when required. Ad requests wait for this handling and are allowed only when the SDK reports canRequestAds. On errors, ads remain unavailable unless the SDK permits requests using previously stored choices. Gameplay is not blocked by a consent error. A form being dismissed is not treated as acceptance.

Settings displays AD PRIVACY OPTIONS when UMP requires a privacy-options entry point, and offers a retry when privacy handling fails. Forms, required choices and available ad serving modes depend on the publisher's AdMob Privacy & messaging configuration, region and SDK state. Publisher action item: configure and verify applicable messages in AdMob Console before release.

The app does not force a non-personalized-ad request flag or set child-directed/under-age ad flags. Do not assume ads are always non-personalized or that canRequestAds means every processing purpose was accepted. Ad serving follows applicable SDK signals and provider configuration; separate Analytics processing is described above.

6. Other network activity and sharing you choose

On Android, the app may check the public Google Play listing for this application's package ID to compare the installed native version with the available store version. This communicates with Google and involves ordinary network metadata, such as IP address. The current check does not attach your scores, coins, local cooldown records or an account identifier. Its update decision is session-only, not a permanently saved lock.

If you select UPDATE NOW, the app opens Google Play or its website, which has its own privacy practices. The check is skipped or fails open when connectivity or valid version information is unavailable.

If you choose the game's Share action, the system share sheet offers a message containing your score to the app or recipient you select. Your chosen sharing service handles the message under its own policy. The game also requests a share_tapped Analytics event.

7. Permissions and device features

The reviewed source uses internet/network connectivity for SDKs, ads and the Play Store check, local storage for saved progress, audio playback for effects, and vibration/haptics for feedback. Android libraries declare network/Wi-Fi state, wake-lock, audio-settings and vibration permissions. Google's SDKs may also declare advertising-ID-related permissions in the final merged app manifest.

Gameplay code does not request camera, microphone, contacts, photo-library or precise-location access. Installed but unused location and image-picker modules declare sensitive permissions; the Android Expo configuration blocks coarse/fine/background location, camera, microphone recording, legacy external storage and media-image/video permissions because the game does not use them. Publisher action item: verify that these blocks are effective in the final merged release manifest. Source configuration is not proof of the final packaged permissions or runtime data collection.

8. Why information is used and shared

Local information is used to save progress, apply settings, manage daily challenges, calculate rewards and enforce the local cooldown. Analytics information is used to understand game use and performance. Advertising information is used by the advertising service for delivery, measurement, analytics and fraud prevention, and potentially personalization depending on configuration and applicable choices.

SDK information is transmitted directly to Google / Firebase / AdMob and may be handled by their affiliates and service providers under their terms. The publisher may view Analytics reports. The absence of a custom backend does not prevent this sharing. Information may be processed outside your country under the providers' arrangements.

No additional publisher-operated data broker or sale mechanism is present in the reviewed game code. This statement is not a legal conclusion that advertising-related processing can never count as a 'sale' or 'sharing' under a particular privacy law. Provider roles, linked services and release configuration must be confirmed before publication.

9. Retention

Local progress and preferences remain until they are overwritten, cleared or removed. The app does not set a general expiry for saved statistics or daily history. A cooldown can expire without deleting its historical state. Device backup/restore settings can also affect whether local data is retained or restored.

Settings > RESET STATISTICS clears scores, total games, total score, coins and daily results. It preserves sound/haptic preferences, completed-game count, cooldown state and the most recent game/reward-claim record. It does not delete information already sent to Firebase or advertising providers.

The publisher reports the following Google Analytics property retention settings: event data retention is 2 months; user data retention is 14 months; reset on new user activity is enabled. These are the corresponding Analytics settings, not universal deletion deadlines for all Google services or all information. Aggregated reports, backups and provider logs can follow different rules. Advertising retention is governed by Google's applicable policies and configuration; no fixed AdMob deletion period is implemented by the app. Publisher action item: review provider-specific retention and linked-service/export settings, and keep this description aligned with the actual property.

10. Your choices

Sound and haptic switches change game preferences; they do not turn off Analytics or advertising collection. Where required by UMP, use Settings > AD PRIVACY OPTIONS to review or change advertising privacy choices. Settings offers RETRY AD PRIVACY when the privacy flow fails. The current app has no dedicated Analytics opt-out or automatic provider-data deletion button.

You can choose not to request a rewarded ad and wait for the cooldown. You can use Android's available advertising/privacy controls, including resetting or deleting the advertising ID where supported. These controls do not necessarily stop all SDK processing.

Android app settings can clear this app's local storage; doing so removes progress and preferences. Uninstalling normally removes local app data, subject to device backups/restores. Clearing or uninstalling the app does not itself send a deletion request for information already held by Google.

Depending on your location and applicable law, you may have rights to request access, correction, deletion or restrictions on processing, or raise concerns with a competent authority. Contact the publisher using the contact details above. What can be identified and acted on depends on the data and provider systems; there is no game account to look up.

Playing offline limits immediate network activity but is not a permanent opt-out, since SDKs may queue data for later transmission.

11. Children's privacy

The publisher's stated intent is a general audience aged 13+, with children not being the target audience. This is a statement of intent, not a finalized age-specific legal declaration. Ages 13–15 may be treated as children in some jurisdictions.

The current app does not implement an age gate, parental-consent flow, or child-directed/under-age advertising configuration. The lack of an account or a form asking for a child's name does not prevent identifiers from being processed by SDKs. Publisher action item: review actual audience, release countries, Google Play target-audience/Families requirements and applicable age-related obligations before distribution; implement any resulting controls and update this policy.

A parent or guardian with concerns should contact the publisher using the privacy contact above. This policy does not claim COPPA, GDPR or other legal compliance.

12. Security

Local data relies on the app's storage and operating-system protections; AsyncStorage is not a dedicated encrypted vault. Google documents encryption in transit for the SDK data described in its disclosures. The game uses HTTPS for its Play Store webpage request.

No storage or transmission method is completely secure. Avoid sharing sensitive information in game-related messages. The publisher must assess release-build security and service-provider access controls; this policy does not guarantee absolute security.
